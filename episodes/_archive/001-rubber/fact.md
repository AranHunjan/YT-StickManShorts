# Episode 001: Why is rubber called rubber?

**Hook:** For hundreds of years, people fixed pencil mistakes with bread.
**Twist:** The material got its English name, *rubber*, because it rubbed out pencil marks.
**Format:** The Hidden Story. **Category:** history / everyday objects.

## Claims and sources (two-source rule)

| # | Claim as said in the script | Sources | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Before rubber erasers, people used bread (breadcrumbs) to erase pencil marks. | Wikipedia "Eraser" and "Edward Nairne" ("Prior to using rubber, breadcrumbs were used as erasers"); Snopes fact-check on bread erasers; Faber-Castell history note | Verified |
| 2 | In 1770 the scientist Joseph Priestley wrote about a substance that wipes pencil marks off paper. | Wikipedia "Eraser" (quotes Priestley's footnote of April 15, 1770: "a substance excellently adapted to the purpose of wiping from paper the mark of black-lead-pencil... sold by Mr. Nairne"); historyofinformation.com entry 1291; Britannica "Rubber" | Verified |
| 3 | Edward Nairne, an instrument maker, sold little cubes of it. | Wikipedia "Eraser" and "Edward Nairne" (cubes at 3 shillings per half-inch cube); Priestley's footnote names Nairne as the seller | Verified |
| 4 | Nairne **claimed** he grabbed it by accident instead of breadcrumbs. | Wikipedia "Eraser" ("According to Nairne..."); Snopes rates the popular story "partly true" | Said as *his claim*, not as fact |
| 5 | The material got the name "rubber" because it rubbed out pencil marks. | Britannica "Rubber" (Priestley gave it the name rubber in 1770 when he found it rubbed out pencil marks); Wikipedia "Eraser" ("rubber" meant any object used for rubbing; it attached to the new material between 1770 and 1778) | Verified |
| 6 | Car tires, rubber bands and rubber ducks carry that name. | Follows from claim 5: they are made of rubber (natural or synthetic) | Verified (definition) |

**Wording guardrails** (from the source check):
- Don't say the word "rubber" was invented in 1770. It already meant "a thing for rubbing".
- Don't present the accident as proven.
- Don't claim bread was the *only* earlier method; wax and stone were also used. The script only says bread was used.
- Avoid "from South America" unless a second source is added.
