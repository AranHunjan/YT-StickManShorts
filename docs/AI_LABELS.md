# AI labels on YouTube, TikTok, Instagram and Facebook

Researched on 2026-11-06 from each platform's own policy pages (sources at the end). Platforms change these rules often, so re-check every few months.

**Short version:** YouTube doesn't need an AI label for Kip N Dex, because it's a cartoon. TikTok, Instagram and Facebook ask for one, because their rules cover realistic AI voices and ours are AI. Label there, as our upload kits already say. Not labeling where a platform requires it risks removal and penalties, and TikTok says a label doesn't affect distribution.

## What we use AI for

- **Voices.** Kip is Chatterbox cloning a voice we designed with Qwen3-TTS; Dex is Qwen3-TTS's "Aiden" preset. Both are realistic-sounding synthetic speech. Neither is a real person's voice.
- **Writing help.** Research, scripts and captions are drafted by Claude and reviewed by the owner.
- **Not AI.** Our own code (Remotion) draws the characters, the animation and the props, and also synthesizes the music and sound effects (`tools/audio/synth.py`).

## Platform by platform

| Platform | What it asks creators to label | Kip N Dex | Upload setting |
|---|---|---|---|
| **YouTube** | Realistic content a viewer could mistake for real: a real person saying or doing something they didn't, altered footage of real events or places, realistic scenes that never happened. Clearly unrealistic content is exempt; YouTube's own example is AI used "in a fully animated video". So is AI used to write a script, title or outline. | Not needed | Altered or synthetic content: **No** |
| **TikTok** | "All AI-generated content that contains realistic images, audio, and video." TikTok's definition of AI content includes cartoon styles, and its examples include AI-generated speech. | Needed, for the voices | AI-generated content: **on** |
| **Instagram, Facebook** | "Photorealistic video or realistic-sounding audio that was digitally created or altered." Meta's policy has no exemption for animation. | Needed, for the voices | AI label: **on** |

### Labels the platforms add themselves

- **YouTube** may label videos made with its own AI tools, videos carrying C2PA "Content Credentials" metadata, and videos its systems detect as AI. You get a notice in YouTube Studio and can usually reverse a wrong label by answering "No". That's not possible when the file carries C2PA metadata or after a manual review.
- **TikTok** labels videos made with its AI effects and uploads carrying Content Credentials. An automatic label can't be removed.
- **Meta** labels content when it finds "industry standard AI indicators", or when the poster discloses.
- **Our files:** `final.mp4` comes out of Remotion and ffmpeg with no Content Credentials, so no platform reads an AI tag from its metadata.
- **One caveat:** Chatterbox stamps an inaudible watermark (Resemble AI's "Perth") into every line it generates, so Kip's lines carry it. None of the platforms' published policies mention reading it. We leave it in place. It's the model maker's way of marking AI speech, and removing it to dodge a label would mean hiding AI use that TikTok and Meta ask us to disclose.

### Does a label cost reach?

- **YouTube:** disclosure "won't limit a video's audience or impact its eligibility to earn money". We don't need it anyway.
- **TikTok:** "Turning on the AI-generated content setting won't affect the distribution of your video", as long as the video follows the Community Guidelines. Since November 2025, though, viewers can choose to see less AI content in their For You feed (Settings, then Content preferences, then Manage topics). TikTok hasn't said exactly how that setting decides what's AI; a label is the obvious signal, so viewers who turned it down may see us less.
- **Meta:** doesn't say.
- **Not labeling** where it's required: TikTok may remove the video; Meta "may apply penalties"; YouTube can add the label itself and, for creators who repeatedly don't disclose, remove content or suspend the Partner Program.

## The bigger risk for money: "inauthentic content"

The label matters less than YouTube's Partner Program rule on inauthentic content, updated on 15 July 2025: mass-produced or repetitive content can't earn money, and YouTube can demonetize the **whole channel**, not just one video. AI tools are allowed; what counts is original, varied work with real creative input. What protects us:

- original characters, animated by our own code instead of an AI video model (`docs/PLAN.md` 6.1);
- each episode's shape is tracked, and the pipeline blocks copies of recent episodes (`tools/episode/variety.py`);
- every fact is verified in a claim ledger, and the owner reviews every episode before it goes up.

## The only way to avoid labels on TikTok and Meta

Human voices: a person records every line. These don't remove the label:

- **Cloning a real voice from samples, even your own.** The lines are still generated by AI. TikTok's rules name this case: a real person "whose image, voice, and/or words are altered or modified by AI". Meta's rule covers any "realistic-sounding audio that was digitally created". (YouTube is fine either way: it exempts animation, and lists cloning your own voice for voice-overs as needing no disclosure.)
- **Converting a recorded line into another voice with AI.** That is "digitally altered" audio.

**Decided 2026-11-06:** the owner offered their and their brother's voices for cloning. Since cloning doesn't remove the labels, we keep the current AI voices, with the labels on TikTok, Instagram and Facebook. Recording the lines themselves would remove them, if that's ever worth the daily time.

## What the pipeline does

- Every `upload.md` carries the three settings lines above. `tools/episode/check_episode.py` warns when one is missing.
- It never strips watermarks or metadata to avoid a label, never says the voices are human, and never answers "No" where a platform's rules say to label.
